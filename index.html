The problem · in their words

Two leaks. One root cause.

Every day, real customers walk in ready to buy and walk out empty-handed. To NorthPeak that isn't a data problem. It's ~$200M a year walking out the door.

Stockouts

A shopper comes in for a product, the shelf is empty. The demand was real, but the sale is lost, and they often buy the rest of the basket somewhere else.

Markdowns

The reverse: product sitting in the wrong stores that won't sell at full price, discounted to clear the shelf. The margin is given away.

Both come from the same thing: inventory in the wrong store at the wrong time, and nobody sees it until it's too late to move.

Technical depth · expect the hard questions

Built to be defended.

"Will it hold at 400 stores?"

Synced UC table → Postgres for low-latency reads; writable tables isolate app state; reverse sync (SCD2) streams decisions back to Delta.

"Was it the model or the data?"

Every recommendation is traceable through inference tables to the inventory and order records behind it, so Hector can show his work in writing.

"How do you know spend is bounded?"

Budgets reject calls over threshold; a guardrail blocks all-data reads; the block itself is captured in the trace as proof it fired.
